The simulators

Seven simulators, all free, all in the browser. Laptops and tablets give the best view. New here? Read the quick guide (PDF). For the physics behind them, read the explainers.

Bloch Simulator

Watch the magnetization vector respond to RF pulses, off-resonance, T1 and T2. Spin echo and inversion recovery in 3D.

Signal Processing

Fourier transforms, sampling and aliasing, and how RF pulse shape sets the slice profile.

Coil Fields & RF Excitation

Biot-Savart fields of loops and Helmholtz pairs, B1+ and flip-angle maps, and how coil geometry sets excitation.

Coil Tuning & Matching

Tune and match a loop coil to the Larmor frequency on a live S11 plot and Smith chart, then watch a patient detune it and lower its Q.

K-space Explorer

A pulse sequence built from real gradients, drawn to scale. Play it and watch each gradient move through k-space while the image builds up.

Head Phantom

A 3D head with real anatomy (the Colin27 brain) in a three-plane viewer. See how it looks on T1, T2, PD and FLAIR.

Why it's built this way

Built by an MRI physicist who teaches, to make the hard parts of MRI physics something you can see and change.

Change it, watch it

Every simulator is live. Move a parameter and the physics responds, so cause and effect are on the screen together.

Predict first

The guided sessions ask for your guess before you run anything. Committing to a guess and checking it is how the physics sticks.

Free, in the browser

Nothing to install and no account. Open a simulator on a laptop or tablet and start.

Early release. These simulators have not been fully quality-checked and are for learning only, not clinical use. Check anything important against your course materials.
