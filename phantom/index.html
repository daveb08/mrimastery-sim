Stylised anatomy, not a segmentation of a real person, with structures placed from approximate standard-atlas (MNI) coordinates scaled to an average adult. Brain: a cortex of even thickness (about 2.7 mm) with the central, pre- and postcentral, Sylvian, cingulate, calcarine and parieto-occipital sulci placed anatomically; the seed varies only the minor folds. C-shaped lateral ventricles, the third and fourth ventricles and the aqueduct; the corpus callosum. Caudate, putamen, pallidum, thalamus, claustrum, hippocampus and amygdala, with the internal, external and extreme capsules between them; the insula. The brain fills the cranial fossae and lies on the tentorium; the cerebellum has lobules, folia and arbor vitae; the brainstem has peduncles, a pons and olives. Head and neck: skull of varying thickness with marrow, the temporalis, frontal, sphenoid, ethmoid and maxillary sinuses, mastoid air, the sella with the pituitary, and the optic chiasm. Orbits with globe, lens, optic nerve and rectus muscles; a nose with septum and turbinates; palate, teeth, tongue and mandible. An airway from the nasopharynx to the trachea; cervical vertebrae with discs around a continuous cord; neck muscles and fat planes. Tissue values are the contrast simulator's 3.0 T table (so WM and GM share T2 = 80 ms and T2-weighted contrast comes from PD and T2* is not used here), plus illustrative muscle, lesion and bone rows; the skull's cortical bone is treated as signal void. Weighted images are ideal spin-echo signal equations, not a full simulation.
