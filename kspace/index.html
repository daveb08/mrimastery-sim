Image from the samples taken so far

The blue line is the path k(t) traced during this TR; the dot is where k is now. During the ADC windows the signal is sampled and k-space fills in. Idle time (the shaded breaks in the diagram) is skipped during playback, except in real time, which plays every millisecond of the scan. The phantom image is noise-free here so that the effect of the sampling stands out.

Slice profiles and the magnetisation each excitation meets

Each gold curve is one slice's excitation profile (|Mxy| after its pulse), simulated with the Bloch equations; click a curve to select that slice. Slices are excited one after another within a TR, so a pulse for slice j also partly tips the edges of its neighbours. That is cross-talk: the blue curve shows the longitudinal magnetisation (Mz) left in the steady state just before the selected slice's excitation, and the dips at the neighbours' positions are the saturation they caused. Dips shrink as the gap grows. Cross-talk usually lowers a slice's signal, but for gradient echo at high flip angles and for EPI it can raise it, because neighbouring pulses saturate the out-of-phase edges of the slice profile; the information panel shows the change as a signed percentage. Dashed red curves (spin echo and fast spin echo) are the refocusing pulses' profiles. Not modelled: magnetisation transfer, stimulated echoes (SE and FSE), and relaxation during the pulses. Interleaved order starts with slice 1 (some scanners start with slice 2 when the number of slices is even).
